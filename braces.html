What is Orthodontics

Orthodontics is a specialized field of dentistry focused on diagnosing, preventing, and correcting misaligned teeth and jaws. Orthodontists use various treatment methods to achieve proper teeth alignment, improve bite function, and enhance facial aesthetics for better oral health and confidence.

Orthodontic Problems

Common orthodontic issues that benefit from professional treatment include:

  • Crowding: Insufficient space for all teeth to fit properly
  • Spacing: Gaps between teeth due to missing teeth or oversized jaw
  • Overbite: Upper front teeth overlap lower teeth excessively
  • Underbite: Lower front teeth are ahead of upper teeth
  • Crossbite: Upper teeth close inside lower teeth on one or both sides
  • Open Bite: Vertical gap between upper and lower teeth when mouth is closed
  • Rotated Teeth: Teeth twisted or rotated out of proper position

Orthodontic Treatment Procedure

The orthodontic treatment journey involves several key steps:

  1. Initial Consultation: Discussion of goals, examination, and X-rays
  2. Treatment Planning: Detailed plan created based on your specific needs
  3. Appliance Placement: Braces or aligners fitted and installed
  4. Regular Adjustments: Monthly appointments to tighten wires and monitor progress
  5. Maintaining Progress: Proper oral hygiene and following care instructions
  6. Retention Phase: Wearing retainers to maintain results after treatment ends

Orthodontic FAQs

How long does orthodontic treatment take?

Most orthodontic treatments take between 18-24 months, though it can vary from 12 to 36 months depending on the complexity of the case and your commitment to the treatment plan.

At what age can you start orthodontic treatment?

Children can start as early as 7-8 years old if early intervention is needed. However, the ideal time is usually between 12-14 years when all permanent teeth have erupted. Adults can receive treatment at any age.

Do braces hurt?

You may experience mild discomfort and pressure after appliance placement or adjustments, usually lasting a few days. This is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Modern braces are designed to minimize discomfort.

Can adults get braces?

Absolutely! Adults can benefit greatly from orthodontic treatment. Your teeth can be moved at any age as long as your gums are healthy. There's no age limit for achieving a beautiful smile.

What should I eat with braces?

Avoid hard, sticky, and chewy foods that can damage braces. Safe foods include soft items like yogurt, mashed potatoes, soup, smoothies, and soft fruits. Cut hard vegetables and meats into small, manageable pieces.

How do I maintain teeth with braces?

Brush after every meal using a soft-bristled toothbrush. Floss daily with special threaders or water flossers. Rinse with mouthwash. Avoid sugary and acidic foods. Regular dental checkups are important to prevent cavities.

What Are Braces?

Braces are dental devices used by orthodontists to straighten crooked teeth, fix misaligned jaws, and improve your smile and bite. They work by applying continuous, gentle pressure over time to move teeth into the correct position.

Main Parts of Braces

  1. Brackets: Small square pieces attached to the front of each tooth. Can be made of metal, ceramic, or plastic.
  2. Archwire: A thin wire that connects all the brackets and controls tooth movement.
  3. Ligatures (Rubber Bands): Small elastic bands that hold the archwire in place.
  4. Bands: Metal rings placed around back teeth for extra support (in some cases).
  5. Elastics: Larger rubber bands that help correct jaw alignment.

How Braces Work

Braces apply constant pressure to your teeth. The bone under your teeth gradually reshapes as your teeth move. Over months (or years), the teeth shift into their proper alignment.

Treatment Duration

Usually lasts between 1 to 3 years, depending on:

  • Severity of the misalignment
  • Type of braces used
  • How well you follow your orthodontist’s instructions

Types of Braces

  1. Metal Braces: Most common and effective. Made of stainless steel. Affordable but very visible.
  2. Ceramic Braces: Tooth-colored, less noticeable. More expensive and can stain easily.
  3. Lingual Braces: Attached behind the teeth (invisible from front). Difficult to clean and can be uncomfortable at first.
  4. Self-Ligating Braces: Don’t use rubber bands. Easier to clean and often need fewer visits.
  5. Clear Aligners (e.g., Invisalign): Transparent plastic trays that fit over teeth. Removable and almost invisible. Require discipline (20–22 hours/day).

Care Tips

  • Avoid sticky or hard foods (chewing gum, popcorn, nuts).
  • Use floss threaders or a water flosser.

Who Is an Orthodontist? (Orthodontist Meaning)

An orthodontist is a dental specialist who focuses on diagnosing, preventing, and correcting misaligned teeth and jaws. They have specialized training beyond general dentistry.

Orthodontists Treat Problems Like:

  • Crooked teeth
  • Overbite & underbite
  • Crossbite
  • Gap between teeth
  • Jaw alignment issues
  • Crowded teeth
  • Open bite
