What Is Dental Cleaning?

Dental cleaning, also known as professional teeth cleaning or oral prophylaxis, is a preventive dental procedure performed by a dentist or dental hygienist to remove plaque, tartar (calculus), and stains from the teeth. Even with regular brushing and flossing at home, some areas of the mouth are hard to clean properly. Dental cleaning helps maintain healthy teeth, strong gums, and fresh breath.

Regular dental cleanings play a vital role in preventing gum disease, tooth decay, bad breath, and tooth loss.

Why Is Dental Cleaning Important?

Dental cleaning is not just about making your teeth look clean; it is essential for long-term oral health.

Key benefits of dental cleaning include:

  • Removes plaque and hardened tartar
  • Prevents gum disease (gingivitis and periodontitis)
  • Reduces the risk of cavities
  • Helps maintain fresh breath
  • Removes surface stains from tea, coffee, smoking, and food
  • Improves overall oral hygiene
  • Helps detect early signs of dental problems

What Happens During a Dental Cleaning?

A professional dental cleaning is a simple and painless procedure. It usually includes the following steps:

1. Oral Examination

The dentist or hygienist examines your teeth and gums using a small mirror to check for cavities, gum inflammation, or other oral issues.

2. Plaque and Tartar Removal

Special dental instruments or ultrasonic scalers are used to remove plaque and tartar buildup from the teeth, especially near the gum line and between teeth.

3. Teeth Polishing

After cleaning, the teeth are polished using a special paste and rotating brush. This helps remove surface stains and smooth the teeth.

4. Flossing

Professional flossing is done to clean between the teeth and remove any remaining debris.

5. Rinsing

Your mouth is rinsed to wash away loosened particles and bacteria.

Types of Dental Cleaning

  1. Routine Dental Cleaning — For people with healthy gums. Recommended every 6 months.
  2. Deep Cleaning (Scaling and Root Planing) — Recommended for patients with gum disease. It cleans below the gum line to remove bacteria and tartar.
  3. Periodontal Maintenance — For patients who have undergone gum disease treatment and need regular follow-up cleanings.

Is Dental Cleaning Painful?

Dental cleaning is usually not painful. You may feel slight pressure or mild discomfort if there is heavy tartar buildup or sensitive gums. For deep cleaning, local anesthesia may be used to ensure comfort.

How Often Should You Get Dental Cleaning?

Most dentists recommend dental cleaning once every 6 months. However, people with gum disease, smoking habits, or other oral health issues may need more frequent cleanings.

Dental Cleaning Before and After Results

  • Cleaner and smoother teeth
  • Brighter smile
  • Fresher breath
  • Healthier gums
  • Reduced gum bleeding

Dental Cleaning Cost

The cost of dental cleaning varies depending on the type of cleaning, location, and clinic. Routine dental cleaning is usually affordable and much cheaper than treating advanced dental problems.

Who Should Get Dental Cleaning?

Dental cleaning is recommended for:

  • Children and adults
  • Smokers
  • People with bad breath
  • Patients with gum problems
  • Individuals wearing braces or dental restorations

Tips to Maintain Clean Teeth After Dental Cleaning

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste
  • Floss every day
  • Use mouthwash as recommended
  • Avoid excessive sugary foods
  • Quit smoking or tobacco use
  • Visit your dentist regularly
