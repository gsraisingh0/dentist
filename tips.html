Top Tips for Healthy Teeth

Learn proven strategies to maintain optimal oral health, prevent cavities, and keep your smile bright and confident.

1

Brush Twice Daily

Brush your teeth for at least 2 minutes, twice a day. Use a soft-bristle toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste to remove plaque and prevent cavities.

2

Don't Forget to Floss

Floss once a day to clean between your teeth where a toothbrush cannot reach. This helps prevent gum disease and bad breath.

3

Limit Sugary Foods & Drinks

Sugary items increase the risk of tooth decay. Reduce consumption of sweets, sodas, and sticky snacks. Choose healthier alternatives like fruits or nuts.

4

Drink Plenty of Water

Water helps wash away food particles and maintains saliva production, which protects your teeth from harmful bacteria.

5

Visit Your Dentist Regularly

Schedule dental check-ups every 6 months. Regular cleanings and exams help detect issues early and keep your teeth healthy.

6

Use Mouthwash

An antibacterial mouthwash reduces plaque, strengthens enamel, and keeps your breath fresh.

7

Avoid Tobacco Products

Smoking or chewing tobacco increases the risk of gum disease, stained teeth, and oral cancer. Avoid these for better oral health.

8

Eat a Tooth-Friendly Diet

Include foods rich in calcium, vitamin D, and phosphorus. Milk, yogurt, leafy greens, cheese, and almonds all strengthen teeth and gums.

9

Replace Your Toothbrush Regularly

Change your toothbrush every 3 months or sooner if the bristles become worn out.

10

Protect Your Teeth

If you play sports, wear a mouthguard. Avoid chewing hard objects like ice or pens to prevent tooth damage.

Scaling & Polishing — What You Need To Know

A concise guide on scaling and polishing: frequency, benefits, what to expect, and aftercare to keep your smile healthy.

  • Frequency: Typically every 6 months; more often if gum disease is present.
  • Benefits: Removes tartar and plaque, prevents gum disease, freshens breath and improves appearance.
  • Aftercare: Avoid staining foods for 24 hours; use a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste.

Quick FAQs

Will it hurt?

Usually not — most patients report only mild sensitivity.

How long does it last?

Results can last months; good home care and routine visits help maintain results.
